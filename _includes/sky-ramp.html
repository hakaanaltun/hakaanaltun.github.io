{% comment %} sky-ramp.html — where the sun is, and what colour the sky is there. The one copy. Before this file the ephemeris lived in three places: a compact inline copy in head.html for Dusk's pre-paint, skyPhase() in js/theme.js, and the instrument ramp in page-sky.html — the first two carrying "keep in sync" comments, which is what a duplicated calculation says about itself. Everything that needs the sun now reads this. It computes and nothing else: no painting, no storage, no DOM. The two callers differ only in what they do with the answer — page-sky.html paints an instrument's four tokens site-sky.html paints the site's full set and both are loaded before first paint, so the sky resolves with no flash. The ephemeris is /twilight/'s, good to about ±0.3°, from the clock and the IANA time zone alone — a coordinate guess, not an almanac, and no location permission is ever asked for. The zones it knows are _data/cities.yml, the one list both this file and /twilight/ render; a zone that is not in it falls back to a nominal equatorial day at the device's UTC offset. The sky has two halves, and SUNRISE is where they meet. From sunrise to sunset it is a DAY: a light paper with dark ink, moving from a pale warm stone while the sun is low, through Fog's grey, to Blueprint's pale blue when it stands high. From sunset to sunrise it is THE RAMP, with light ink: the deep night stops, then a grey-violet twilight that reaches the horizon. Its brightest ground retains at least 6.5:1 for body ink and 5.5:1 for the site's muted tier. Until 23 October 2026 the ramp ran on into a daytime slate with the same light ink, and the author found the day too dark. It could not be made lighter: light ink holds the dimmest tier only on a ground about as dark as that slate, and a ground passing from dark to light runs through a grey on which neither ink reads. So the sky turns once at sunrise and once at sunset, as a phone's own dark mode does. The slate stayed for THE EVENING. The same day the author found that the night came down too soon after sunset, and asked for the slate to hold from sunset until EVENING_ENDS, 21:00 on the reader's own clock. It eases into the ramp over the half hour before, so the night is whole at nine. The evening is the clock's, not the sun's: nine is late in winter and early in summer by the sun, and the same hour of the evening either way. {% endcomment %}
