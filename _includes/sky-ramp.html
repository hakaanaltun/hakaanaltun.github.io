{% comment %} sky-ramp.html — where the sun is, and what colour the sky is there. The one copy. Before this file the ephemeris lived in three places: a compact inline copy in head.html for Dusk's pre-paint, skyPhase() in js/theme.js, and the instrument ramp in page-sky.html — the first two carrying "keep in sync" comments, which is what a duplicated calculation says about itself. Everything that needs the sun now reads this. It computes and nothing else: no painting, no storage, no DOM. The two callers differ only in what they do with the answer — page-sky.html paints an instrument's four tokens site-sky.html paints the site's full set and both are loaded before first paint, so the sky resolves with no flash. The ephemeris is /twilight/'s, good to about ±0.3°, from the clock and the IANA time zone alone — a coordinate guess, not an almanac, and no location permission is ever asked for. THE RAMP is the twilight page's colours, deepened above −2° so the site's text hierarchy fits on them. The instruments carry two text tiers and the original daytime slate held them at 4.52:1, two hundredths over the AA floor; the site carries four, and on that same slate the brightest possible ink tops out at 4.81:1, so its dimmest tier could not exist at all. Each lit stop is therefore scaled along its own hue until white ink reads 7.4:1 on it — which puts the muted tier at 6.2:1, matching Dark's own dimmest text (6.17:1). Same colours, more depth. The night stops are untouched: they were already at 14.7:1. {% endcomment %}
