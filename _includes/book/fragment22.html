The Cove

The cove, when I reached it, was exactly as beautiful as he’d said it would be. The cliff faced west. The water was clear. The sun was an hour from setting.

I sat on a rock near the edge and looked out at the sea. Lena’s morning notification floated back to me: Your sleep quality last night was 43% below baseline. Lena recommends postponing essential decisions today. I’d dismissed it before we left. I’d dismissed my own warning years ago, the night before that café—the night I called Deniz. “Konuşmamız lazım,” I had said, and she had agreed too easily. I had told myself to wait. I hadn’t.

Lena buzzed: You seem to be alone. Would you like me to adjust the dinner reservation to one person?

I stared at the notification for a long time. And then Deniz’s sentence came back to me—the one she’d said in that café years ago, the one I’d walked out of thinking I’d understood. We’re children pretending to be adults. That’s all we’ve ever been. I had not heard that the sentence had three subjects, not two.

Then I typed the only honest thing I’d said all day:

Don’t do anything. Just wait.

Lena paused. Then:

Condition undefined. Queuing request.

Two years of learning me, and this was the one thing she couldn’t parse. She’d handled just wait before, that afternoon with the syllabus, with a simple understood.

The sun went down. The water turned from blue to gold to black. Şafak was in a town whose name I didn’t know.

I sat there until the stars came out. The car, parked behind me in the dark, waited for a command I no longer knew how to give.
