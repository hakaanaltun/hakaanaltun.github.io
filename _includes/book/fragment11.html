Gölgem

Moris is already in every room I walk into. I close the bathroom door behind me. A few minutes later he’s on the bath mat, studying me as I dry my hair. The doors stay open in this apartment. He sees to it.

When I’m working, he climbs onto the desk and rubs his cheek along the side of the laptop, talking the whole time. Then he walks across the keyboard. He sits down on the trackpad and looks at me.

I look up from the page and he’s on the back of the sofa, tail twitching, watching a sparrow on the railing. The sparrow has personally insulted him.

I look up twenty minutes later and he’s at my feet, asleep. He moves when I look away. And sometimes he turns and gives me a look that says I have seen everything in this room a thousand times and I am still surprised by your face.

I laugh out loud. The laughter comes unbidden, sudden as a sneeze. He is on the kitchen table now, asleep, one paw over his eyes.
