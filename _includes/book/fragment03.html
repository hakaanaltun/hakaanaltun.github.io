A Nostalgia Named Gravity

Drifting forever, I had nothing to hold onto. I moved through the dark, a sound passing through a room with no walls, and the moving was the only thing I had left.

Symmetry was dead. I thought I alone had remained. The emptiness was so wide it could not even end me.

Then I saw the others. The leftovers, like me. We were all that remained from the great cancellation, scattered across distances.

One was moving away from another, and as it moved, something in it strained backward, toward the one it was leaving. Don’t let me go, it seemed to say.

Some of them heard.

They reached.

Three pieces, drifting apart, found each other and held. The grip was strong and intimate. The space between them stopped expanding. Where there had been three solitudes, there was now a single warmth, and the warmth had a form.

These holdings would take different names.

I had one name for all of it.

Nostalgia.

The longing of the leftover for the leftover. A pull older than space itself, reaching across whatever distance the universe could put between two things, and saying: come back.

I felt it in my own going. I had been drifting too, and now I was drifting toward something.

I let myself go.

I held.

And in the holding, the cold around me thinned. Long afterward, beneath a blanket, I would listen to another body breathe. Other holdings happened in other places. Pieces gathered into knots. The knots found each other and made larger knots, and the larger knots, burning under their own weight, became the first stars.

The dark filled with small fires, each one a holding.

We all whispered.

Hold on. Outside is the drift. Here, between us, is the place we came from.

And we held.
