You Can Finish It

A young man called to me outside the ferry terminal.

“Hakan Hocam.”

I knew the face before he reached me, though he was older now.

“How are you?”

“Good, hocam. You?”

“Still here.”

“I heard.”

He laughed. The shoulder lifted first. I had seen that movement from behind a desk. For a second, a classroom returned around him, with his coat hanging from the back of a chair and dripping onto the floor.

“You were going into engineering,” I said.

“Translation Studies.”

“Yes. Of course.”

“Kerem was engineering.”

“Kerem.”

The name opened another face. We stood to one side as people moved around us toward the turnstiles.

He told me where he worked. I followed every sentence.

He glanced toward the ferries.

“I still hear you sometimes,” he said. “When I stop in the middle of a sentence. You used to say, ‘Mert, if you started it, you can finish it.’”
