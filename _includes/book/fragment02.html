The First Disobedience

I am calling it the first because I have to call it something. The word is a courtesy. There was no first. Every going was its own going, every ending the only ending, and the almost in me had been growing heavier across countless beginnings before one of them became this one.

I began.

I saw him across from me. The face was mine, reversed. The pull came as it always came, and I went.

And then the echo arrived.

It came from beyond time, from trillions of years ahead, and it found me here, at the edge of the going. It came as a memory I had not yet earned. The sound of my own voice, older than anything, reaching me across a distance the universe had yet to invent.

Disturb the balance. Defile Symmetry.

There were no words. I heard it the way a stone might hear the hand that throws it.

I held.

The pull was still there. He was still there, the face that was mine, coming toward me at the same speed, obeying. The law was still the law. And I—for the first time in any of the goings, in any of the nows—did not.

I swerved.

Symmetry broke.

The sky went white. Every duel, every matched pair, flared at once and emptied into a single sea of light.

The light should have taken me too. It did not.

Then the haze cleared. And the books did not balance.

A debt no one had paid. Alone. Me.

Nothing pulled. Nothing would ever pull again.

I trembled into the silence. Why did I remain?

Across the dark, others had swerved too, though I did not know it then.

Each of us believed we were the first.
