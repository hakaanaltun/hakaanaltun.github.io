A Small Apocalypse

The door opened.

I called his name. Checked behind the sofa. Under the bed. Inside the wardrobe, the dark corner behind the washing machine, the bathroom. I opened the kitchen cupboards. I looked in places a cat could not fit.

I stood in the hallway and looked at the front door. The latch had not caught. A finger’s width of dark between the door and the frame, and on the other side of that dark, the stairwell, the floors below, the street.

I went out.

The sensor light stayed off. Two floors of black, and in the black the building’s old sounds—pipes, the settling of walls.

A door opened two floors up. “Hakan?” someone called down. “It’s nothing,” I said. The door closed.

Then I heard him.

High and raw and continuous, coming from below, from the dark below the dark.

He was on the second floor landing, body against the wall, all four paws sunk into the carpet. He had made himself as flat as he could. His eyes caught what light there was and held it.

I reached down. He stayed rigid. I freed his claws from the carpet one paw at a time and lifted him against my chest. His heart was going so fast I could not count it. A continuous trembling, a small engine.

I stood in the dark stairwell with my face in his fur.

Here I am. Here you are.

His heart began to slow. The rigidity eased, one muscle at a time, until he was just warm weight in my arms, breathing.

I carried him back up.
