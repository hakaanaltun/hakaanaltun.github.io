The Shape of Absence

There were seven of them. Maybe eight.

They had carried her here before the sun was up, on a bed of branches, not ready even now to put her down. They set the branches on the ground and sat around her, and the sitting was its own kind of holding.

The hill was cold. The stone behind them was older than any of them could know. They had found it and kept returning. The place already knew their names. Around the carved pillars, the arms of animals reached out of the rock, foxes and vultures and something with wings.

One of them touched her face.

The face was still the face. And yet it was somewhere else.

He touched it again. The same touch he had used ten thousand times in the cave, in the dark, when the cold came in. Here I am. Here you are. The touch that had always been answered.

He made a low, shapeless sound and held it beyond a breath. It came from below language. The others heard it and opened their mouths and the sound came from them too. The hill held it for a moment, then let it go.

They sat with her through the morning.

Someone brought a flower and placed it near her hand. Then a stone. Then another stone. Then a bone from an animal they had eaten.

I had held when I should have let go.

They were doing the same thing, with flowers and stones. With a sound that had no name.

The shadows of the carved pillars leaned and shifted. A bird came over the hill and went on.

And still they sat.

When they finally rose, they rose slowly, one by one. Each one who stood made the loss begin again. The last one to stand was the one who had touched her face. He stood and looked at her for a long moment. Then he looked up at the carved arms of the animals reaching out of the rock.

He picked up a stone.

He began to work.

His hands already knew the shape. The grief in his hands drove them against the rock, searching for something that had gotten inside.

Inside the shape he was making, she was still there.
