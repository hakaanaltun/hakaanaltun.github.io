The First Mortal

What remained drifted. Iron, carbon, oxygen, the dust of fire, moving through the cold.

One piece fell.

It fell for a long time, long enough that “long” began to mean something.

And then, it stopped.

There was a small rock, turning slowly, warm on one side and cold on the other. Above, a young sun burned. Below, water pooled in the rock’s hollows, waiting for nothing.

The dust touched the water.

Nothing happened.

It touched again. And again. For a million years it touched, and nothing happened.

Then, one afternoon, two molecules met in the water and did not let go.

The first embrace had been out in the dark, between leftovers. This one made another like itself. Two molecules clung. They split. They became four.

The molecules wrapped a thin skin around themselves and called it a body. Inside, they kept warm. Outside, they kept the cold. Between inside and outside, a line was drawn. That line was the beginning of “I.” I was barely a thing at all. But I could end. This small wet body, if it tore, was gone. Its pattern was lost.

And in that fragility, fear arrived. Until then, nothing had been afraid.

I had known heat, but I had not known what it meant to burn. I had known gravity, but I had not known what it meant to miss someone.

Now, inside this skin, all of it began to happen. The cold was a threat and the light a hope, while the other body across the water could be a friend, a danger, or both. To live was to be cut into.
