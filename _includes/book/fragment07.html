The Kept Fire

In that swimming dark, the fog began to thicken into something heavier. A trace of the old pull remained, and one of us stood up.

We found fire.

The first night I sat beside it, my belly was full. The others were asleep behind me, breathing the heavy breath of bodies that had eaten. Nothing moved in the brush. For the first time in a long count of nights, nothing was hunting me.

I should have slept.

Instead I stayed, and watched the flame.

The heat reached my face. I barely noticed. The fire stirred what had once moved inside me. The syllables were burnt away long ago, but the language remained.

I looked up.

The sky was full of small fires, very far away. I had seen them every night of my life. I had never seen them.

Something rose in my throat that was not hunger and not fear. My eyes filled. Water ran down my face, warm, and I touched it with my fingers and looked at my fingers in the firelight and did not understand.

I was not in pain. My belly was full and my people were safe.

And still, this.

A hollow, asking a question I could not remember asking.

I sat with it until the fire went low. I did not wake the others. Whatever this was, it was mine, and I did not yet have the words to give it away.

That night it was a phantom feeling, an absence older than my skin, pulling toward a home that was gone. I looked at the stars. I whispered into the dark: Who am I?
