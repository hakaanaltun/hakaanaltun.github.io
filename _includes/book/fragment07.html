The Kept Fire

In that swimming dark, the fog began to thicken. A trace of the old pull remained, and one of us stood up.

We found fire.

The first night I sat beside it, my belly was full. The others were asleep behind me, breathing the heavy breath of bodies that had eaten. Nothing moved in the brush. For the first time in a long count of nights, nothing was hunting me.

I should have slept.

Instead I stayed, and watched the flame.

The heat reached my face. I barely noticed. The fire stirred what had once moved inside me. The syllables were burnt away long ago, but the language remained.

I looked up.

The sky was full of small fires, very far away. I had seen them every night of my life. I had never seen them.

Something rose in my throat that had nothing to do with hunger or fear. My eyes filled. Warm water ran down my face. I touched it and looked at my fingers in the firelight, unable to understand.

I was not in pain. My belly was full and my people were safe.

And still there was this hollow, asking a question I could not remember asking.

I kept it until the fire went low. I did not wake the others. Whatever this was, it was mine, and I did not yet have the words to give it away.

That night it was a phantom feeling. I looked at the stars. I whispered into the dark, Who am I?
