Moris

I forgot I had a cat.

It was his first night in the apartment. He was six months old, a tiny, terrified shadow that had spent most of the day hiding in some invisible corner of the house. He came out briefly to eat, keeping low to the ground, then disappeared again. I had spent the day getting the apartment ready for him, buying bowls and carrying litter, the things you do to welcome another life into your own. But by the time I turned off the lights and got into bed, I was exhausted. My brain discarded the new data. I fell asleep as a man living alone.

Around 3:00 a.m., I woke with the feeling that someone was watching me.

The room was pitch black. I opened my eyes and saw two massive, glowing green eyes right in front of my face. For a moment, they were all I could see.

His name is Moris, and he is entirely black. In a dark room, he becomes the dark. He had climbed onto the bed without making a sound and was inspecting my sleeping face.

For a few seconds, I was terrified. My heart started pounding, and I pulled back before I understood what I was looking at.

Then I took a breath. The void blinked, made a small sound, and sat down on my blanket.

I lay back down, my pulse slowly returning to normal. He curled into a tight shape near my feet and went to sleep.

I stayed awake a little longer. The refrigerator hummed, and a whistle rose from the street below. I lay there sorting the day behind me, rehearsing the one ahead. Moris just existed. He breathed slowly and evenly, with the ease of a creature with no unfinished business.

I listened to that rhythm for a while.

Then I closed my eyes and let the dark simply be the dark.

I started calling him gölgem.

Moris is already in every room I walk into. I close the bathroom door behind me. A few minutes later he’s on the bath mat, studying me as I dry my hair. The doors stay open in this apartment. He sees to it.

When I’m working, he climbs onto the desk and rubs his cheek along the side of the laptop, talking the whole time, before walking across the keyboard to sit on the trackpad and look at me.

I look up from the page and he’s on the back of the sofa, tail twitching, watching a sparrow on the railing. The sparrow has personally insulted him.

I look up twenty minutes later and he’s at my feet, asleep. He moves when I look away. And sometimes he turns and gives me a look that says I have seen everything in this room a thousand times and I am still surprised by your face.

I laugh out loud. The laughter comes unbidden, sudden as a sneeze. He is on the kitchen table now, asleep, one paw over his eyes.
