The Kitchen

I am sitting at the kitchen table, under the yellow, hazy light of the hood. There is a strange, heartwarming comfort that light gives you at this hour of the night. Under it, all the secrets of the world seem briefly solvable. For a moment—and it is only a moment—I do not remember sitting down. The chair is under me, the tea in front of me, but the ordinary sequence of walking to the kitchen and filling the kettle has dropped out—a missing stair in the dark. I look at my hands on the table. They are mine. Whatever just happened did not happen. I shake it off. The hum in my head won’t stop, and tonight even the yellow light cannot silence it. All the questions and answers are tangled in the dimness.

The refrigerator runs its low constant note. Through the open window, I hear the long, flat sound of a ship out on the Marmara.

Moris is curled up sleeping at the other end of the table, in his own little kingdom. Every now and then, his soft paws twitch; he is probably chasing a bird in his dream that he will never catch.

My tea has gone cold. I register it as a minor betrayal.

I think about that bird Moris is chasing in his dream. And that old cartoon pops up in my mind.

Wile E. Coyote, whose whole existence is dedicated to catching the Road Runner. In one episode, he achieves the impossible. He catches the bird. At that moment, the world stops. Coyote turns to the screen, pulls out a sign, and looks at the audience. The sign reads:

“Alright, wise guys, you always wanted me to catch him. Now, what do I do?”

I smile.

I remember a physics paper I read the other night.

“The future can shape the past.”

Just then, blockchain technology comes to mind. The sealed blocks. The chain that cannot be rewritten.

One reality where the past has not been written yet. Another where it cannot be unwritten.

I pause.

Outside, a car passes on the wet street.

And yet that resemblance sticks to the corner of my mind like a stain that won’t come out.
