Mud and Blood

One by one, every fire I had been was put out behind me, and the dark closed in. Memory withered. Hunger took its place. For billions of years, the hollow ache in me was my only compass.

I drifted in the darkness of a primordial ocean. A thousand thousand times, I was small and moving through warm water. Something passed above me. A shadow with the shape of hunger. I went still. The water around me went still. The shadow stopped. It hung there, deciding. My whole body became a held breath. Every cell that was me trembled. The water moved again.

Other times the hunger went first. A smaller body turned in the warm water, and I was already turning with it. The ache drew me down after it. When it was over, the ache was quiet for a while.

Each time I died, the terror arrived as a stranger. The very elements I had once forged—iron, carbon, oxygen—returned as teeth and claws and venom to tear my flesh. The dust of dead stars shredding the dust of dead stars. I bled.

Inside the bloody churn of the ocean, there were no names. Only the long edge of the knife.
