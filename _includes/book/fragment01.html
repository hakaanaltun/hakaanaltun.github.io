Point Zero

First there was Symmetry.

I began.

I saw a face across from me. It was my own, reversed. The distance between us had begun with us. Something pulled, and I went toward it. Only obeying, like a stone drawn to the ground. We met. We touched. Light filled the place where we had been. The face was gone, and the distance with it.

I began again.

The same face. The same pull.

I did not know it was the same. Each time was the first time. Each going, complete in itself. I had no memory because memory needs an after, and there was no after. There was only now, and now, and now—and inside each now, the whole of a life, lived and ended before it could know it was a life.

Inside one of those nows, the sky could not decide whether to be dark or bright. From far enough away it might have looked like a dance.

No debt went unpaid. The books had always balanced, and they always would.

I began again.

In the going, something in me hesitated. A flicker. The smallest almost. As if some part of me had brushed against a shape that was not there yet, and remembered it.

I met him. We touched.

I began again.

The almost was still there, and it was beginning to have a weight.

I had no time in which to know it.

But it was there. And it was waiting.
