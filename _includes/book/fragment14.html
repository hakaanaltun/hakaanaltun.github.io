The Curse

I turn my eyes to the exam papers in front of me on the table.

I’m holding colored pens. The classic red one stays in the drawer. I don’t like the way it screams “You are wrong!” all over the paper. Mistakes shouldn’t look like casualties of war.

But everything becomes mechanical when the answer key is right here. The student reads the question, sweats, thinks, tries to find meaning. For them, that question is an adventure. An unknown.

For me? Just “A, C, D, B.”

I know the answers. And the paper holds no magic for me.

People sometimes find teaching boring. “You teach the same things every day,” they say.

I sigh.

They don’t know that the best part of teaching is who you teach. The students change, and so does the spirit of the time. The subjects don’t have to.

I put the pens down.

The fluffy ball at the end of the table moves. Moris lifts his head and looks at me. Pure curiosity in his eyes.

He doesn’t know if the sun will rise tomorrow, or if the bird he saw today will land on the windowsill again.

His curiosity makes him more alive than me.

I return to those two thoughts. A faint old spark catches.

“Could it be?” I say. Then I shrug.

“Saçmalama, Hakan,” I mumble. “You’ve turned into a conspiracy theorist. One is just a digital ledger. The other is subatomic particles. Git yat.”

I stand up and reach for the light switch.

All of it—the two thoughts, the question, the half-answer—buried in darkness with a mechanical click.

Maybe the answer is there. But not tonight.

I follow Moris toward the bedroom in the dark. The question goes with me, postponed to tomorrow.

There is class in the morning.

I have a lot to tell those variable souls waiting for me.
