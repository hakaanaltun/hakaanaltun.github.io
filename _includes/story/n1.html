The notice had gone up weeks ago, on the street door. Nobody was behind that door to find it. One of the old neighbors had seen it in passing and rung his mother, and she had rung him the same evening. A photograph came after the call. The notice bore a seal and a block number, with a date for demolition. All of it was at a height that had once been out of his reach. The date was this week.

His mother meant to be there for it. She had said so in the flat voice she kept for settled things. She would stand at the corner while the block came down, to the last of it, and no one was going to move her somewhere kinder. He would be beside her when the machines came at first light. That was the whole of the trip.

He had booked the 7:45 that same night, the only flight that day. It would land after dark. The bag had stood by the door since, with a change of clothes and the prescription folder inside. The key had gone across the landing to the neighbor.

He had taken two days of leave, with the weekend after. The morning stayed open. A record played low while he opened the photographs on his laptop. The street was in some of them, from before the trees filled out. The door was still without its notice. The prints had been at the house; those he had taken with him had been lost in later moves. That week he called home to ask after an old photograph of the two of them. His mother looked. There was none.

His father had been a year gone.

The block had been coming down for longer than that. He had had no say in it; there was only the date, and he had taken leave for the last of it.

The interior doors had glass then, small panes in a wooden grid. A ball indoors on a wet afternoon, one pane gone to the floor. The scolding came from the kitchen and reached every room in the flat. Some of it was still there to be heard.

At one the phone rang. It was the general manager’s own line, on a day the office knew his desk was empty. The work could not wait, and there was no one to hand it to. There never was.

The bag came with him to the office. By four it was done.

The train from his corner would have run straight to the airport. It was five minutes’ walk, with no change after that. From the office side the water lay between, and the short way over it was the ferry. The slope down to the pier ran along the park.

The park.
