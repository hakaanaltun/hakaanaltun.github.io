The notice had gone up weeks ago, on the street door. No one lived there now. An old neighbor had seen it and called his mother. She called him that evening and sent a photograph afterward. The notice showed the block number and a demolition date beneath an official seal. It hung at a height that had once been out of his reach. The date was this week.

His mother meant to be there. She said she would stand at the corner until the block was down. He knew from her voice that she would not be persuaded to leave. He would stand beside her when the machines came at first light.

He had booked the 7:45 flight that same night. It was the only flight that day and would land after dark. His bag had stood by the door since, packed with a change of clothes and the prescription folder. He had left the key with the neighbor across the landing.

He had taken two days of leave before the weekend. That morning he played a record low and opened the photographs on his laptop. Some showed the street before the trees filled out. The door had no notice on it then. The prints had been at the house, and those he had taken with him had been lost in later moves. That week he had called his mother about an old photograph of himself and his father. She had looked for it but could not find it.

His father had been dead a year.

The demolition had been planned before that. He had had no say in it. Now there was a date, and he had taken leave to be there at the end.

The interior doors had small glass panes in wooden frames. One wet afternoon he had broken a pane while playing ball indoors. Someone had scolded him from the kitchen. He could still hear the scolding.

At one the phone rang. It was the general manager, though the office knew he was on leave. The work could not wait. There was no one to cover for him; there never was.

He took the bag to the office. By four it was done.

From home he would have walked five minutes to the train and gone straight to the airport. From the office, the water lay between. The ferry was quickest. The slope down to the pier ran along the park.

The park.
