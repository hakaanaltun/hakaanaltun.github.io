His mother would be at the corner by first light and stand there to the end, whether or not there was a shoulder beside hers. He could make no sense of arriving afterward, when all that remained was a heap. So he stopped here.

The way home was shorter for being known. He took the train to his corner, walked the five minutes home and collected the key from across the landing. The tree at the corner stood in new leaf.

It was an evening like the evenings when there had been no going. He put the kettle on; the record was where he had left it. From the outside nothing marked it.

The 4:15 stayed on the board and left with the seat empty.

At first light the phone lay on the table, face up. He made no call, and none came.

Somewhere on the far side the machines started, and did not carry this far.
