At the top of the slope the phone went again. The general manager said it had gone through. The other side had refused to budge; how had he managed it?

“Being there was enough.”

The 4:45 was the last ferry that would get him to the flight.

School had let out at four. Students in uniform came down the slope toward the crowded bus stop across the road.

Two children had stopped under the chestnuts beyond the car park. The taller one held out a notebook and shook it.

“You didn’t do my homework,” the taller child said quietly.

The smaller child stood with empty hands, looking at the notebook.

“I’m never doing it.”

On the second syllable of never the taller child began to tear. The spine split, and pages were torn out by the handful. They fell beneath the chestnuts’ new leaves. Before they had settled, the taller child began to cry.

The crying was real. It carried up toward the gate with a claim that the smaller child had torn the notebook.

A teacher came out at the sound and started down.

The smaller child did not look up.

The teacher would hear the account at the gate. Whatever was decided, the other children would hear about it afterward.

From the gate, the teacher could see the crying child and the torn pages. He had seen whose hands had torn them. He had a ferry to catch.

A teacher was already here. The school would sort it out. His mother would be at the corner at first light.

He kept walking down the slope. It took an effort to keep his head turned toward the water.

He had eleven minutes until the 4:45, and the walk to the pier took nine. The teacher was halfway across the grass.

Cross the grass.

Make the ferry.
