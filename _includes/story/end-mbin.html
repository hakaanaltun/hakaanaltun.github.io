The terminal thinned to cleaners and a long fluorescent quiet. He lay across a row of joined seats with the bag for a pillow. The boarding hour would not come.

Around three the arithmetic ran on its own. Had he stayed off the grass, he would have made the ferry and the flight. He would have spent the night on the far side and gone to the corner with his mother at first light. Instead he sat under the terminal lights; she would stand there alone.

Once, under the lights, the park came back. The pages lay white under the new leaves, and he could see the hands that had held them. What the gate had settled he never learned. Given the hour again, he would have crossed the same.

At a quarter to four he went to the gate. His seat was over the wing.

The runway came up grey, a little after six, first light already on the hangars.

A taxi took the shore road into a city awake ahead of itself. At the corner stood the plane tree and the shuttered grocery. Where the block had stood, a barrier was folded back before a heap going dark with damp.

It was over.

His mother stood alone before the rubble, hands behind her back, in the place she had kept to the end.

She did not turn at the step behind her. She was already looking at something on the ground.

He looked first at her, then to where she was looking.

A photograph, face up. A man stood on a step with a child at knee height beside him. Behind them was the doorframe with its first pencil line.

The bag stayed on his shoulder.

There was dust on the new leaves.
