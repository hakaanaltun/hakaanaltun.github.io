The coast fell away below the wing.

In the window he could see the cabin reflected against the dark. He thought of the flat.

Once a year his father stood him against the doorframe and laid a hand flat on his head, pressing down his hair. He made a pencil mark and wrote the year beside it in small figures. Each mark was higher. His father had been dead a year now. The last mark was level with his own shoulder. By first light the frame and its marks would be down in the street.

He remembered getting up before dawn on a holiday morning. His new clothes had been laid over the chair the night before. Later he left for university in another city with a bag heavier than this one. His mother stood at the door. A hundred metres on he turned and saw her at the window. He raised an arm, and she raised hers.

After midterms, the university had shut for a fortnight. He told his parents he had another week of exams and would come straight after. He spent that week in the city and could not later remember what he had done. When he got home, his parents were quiet over breakfast. His grandfather had been buried a week earlier. They had kept it from him. He had exams, they said.

Other times he had taken a call in a corridor or been unable to move a date. The last time, there had been no lie and no one left to spare him the news. He reached the ward a day late. The bed was already stripped.

All of it came back before the seatbelt light went out.

Then an announcement came, and the plane began to descend.
