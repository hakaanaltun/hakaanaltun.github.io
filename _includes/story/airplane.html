The coast fell away below the wing.

Nothing came in the window but the cabin and, past it, the dark. The flat came instead.

His father, and the measuring. Once a year his father stood him against the doorframe and laid a hand flat on the crown of his head to take the hair out of the count. He made a pencil mark where the hand had been and wrote the year beside it small. Each mark came higher than the last, up toward his father’s hand. That hand had been a year gone now. The last mark stood level with his own shoulder, and by first light the frame and its marks would be down in the street.

On a holiday morning he had been up before it was light, the new clothes stiff over the chair since the night before. Later came the first leaving, for the other city and the university, with a bag heavier than this one. His mother stood at the door. A hundred metres on he turned to check, and she was at the window. He raised an arm, and she raised hers.

The first absence had a lie folded in it. Midterms were done and the university shut for a fortnight. He told home he had another week of exams and would come straight after. He gave the free week to that city and to nothing he could name later. When he came, his parents sat quiet over a breakfast that would not lift. His grandfather had been a week in the ground. They had kept it from him: he had exams, they said, and no sense in his sitting them in mourning.

There were other times he had not been there. A call came in a corridor; a date could not be moved. The last time needed no lie and left no one to spare him. He reached the ward the day after. The bed was already stripped.

All of it came back in the time the seatbelt light took to go dark.

Then came the announcement, and the descent.
