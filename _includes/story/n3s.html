The doors of the 4:45 closed on time. The airport lights came on across the water as daylight faded. The white wake took a long time to settle behind the ferry.

He reached the gate and boarded. His seat was over the wing.

They landed after dark. The city lights stretched along the water.

His mother’s building stood back from a newer road. The lift worked, and her name was typed on a card by the door. Dinner was already on when he rang. They ate at the small table. The television was on low in the other room; neither watched it.

Neither mentioned the block.

She talked instead about working on a factory line in Germany and sending money home. The two families had arranged her marriage while she was still there. She had come back a bride to a door she had never seen and lived there for forty years.

She remembered a shopping trip with his father. He had sat in the front passenger seat while she and her sister sat behind him. No one had told him which sister he was to marry. Years later he had told her what he had been thinking. She repeated his words at the table: “The whole way, I hoped it was you.”

He mentioned the photograph again, the one of himself and his father, lost in the moves years ago. His mother had heard it before. She passed the bread.

They stayed at the table after the plates were cleared. He felt a weight on his chest through the tea. It was still there later, when he lay in the small room with the light out.
