The doors of the 4:45 closed on the minute. Across the water the airport came on light by light, and the last of the day went with the crossing. The wake ran white behind, and was a long time settling.

He reached the gate and boarded. His seat was over the wing.

The runway came up in the dark, and the city after it, strung along the water.

The building where his mother lived now stood back from a newer road. The lift worked, and her name was typed on a card by the door. Dinner was already on when he rang. They ate at the small table, the television low in the other room for a company neither of them watched.

The block was not mentioned. The door on it stayed shut.

She talked instead about the factory line in Germany and the money sent home. The marriage had been settled between the two families while she was still there. She had come back a bride to a door she had never seen, and stayed behind it forty years.

One story had his father in it. They had been shopping, and the car was full. His father sat in the front right seat, his mother and her sister behind. No one had yet told him which sister he was to marry. Years into the marriage he had told her what the ride had been, and she said it now at the small table, in his words: “The whole way, I hoped it was you.”

Somewhere in the talk the photograph came up again. It had been lost in the moves years back and never found; without it the home was only in his head now. She had heard it before and let it pass again, and passed the bread.

The evening did not lift when the plates were cleared. It sat on his chest through the tea and was still there when the light in the small room went out.
