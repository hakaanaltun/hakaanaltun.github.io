Twenty steps onto the grass and the ferry was already lost.

The teacher had come down to the chestnuts. The account was being given again, with the torn pages for evidence.

He told the teacher what he had seen. It took a minute, maybe less. The teacher listened, then looked past the one still crying to where the notebook had come apart and to the hands that had held it.

The crying went on a moment out of habit. No more words came.

By then the pier had its gates shut and the 4:45 was a length out, turning.

He took a taxi around the water. The driver understood the hour on his ticket. He tried the shoulder where the lane ran out and went through an amber late. It was not enough. The shore road held its evening traffic, and the water stayed uncrossed to his right the whole way.

There was still someone behind the check-in desk. The 7:45 had gone twenty minutes back; the board had already dropped it. The next flight left at 4:15 and landed at 6:04, first light on the far side.

First light was when the machines came, and when his mother would take her place at the corner.

Board the 4:15.

Return home.
