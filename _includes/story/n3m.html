Twenty steps onto the grass and he knew he would miss the ferry.

The teacher was beside the chestnuts. The taller child was telling it again, still crying, with the torn pages scattered nearby.

He told the teacher what he had seen. It took a minute, maybe less. The teacher listened, then looked past the crying child to where the notebook had been torn and the hands that had held it.

The crying went on a moment longer, but no more words came.

By then the gates at the pier were shut. The 4:45 was already turning away.

He took a taxi around the water. The driver knew the time of his flight and tried the shoulder where the lane ran out. He went through an amber late. It was not enough. Traffic held them on the shore road, with the water to his right the whole way.

There was still someone at the check-in desk. The 7:45 had left twenty minutes ago and was gone from the board. The next flight left at 4:15 and landed at 6:04, first light on the far side.

First light was when the machines came, and when his mother would take her place at the corner.

Board the 4:15.

Return home.
