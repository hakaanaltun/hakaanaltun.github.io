They came to the corner together as the grey came up, his mother a step ahead where the pavement narrowed. They took a place across from the barrier while the machine woke.

She stood as she had said she would, hands behind her back, watching the wall.

The arm swung and the top floor gave. It came down by rooms, a near wall opening on wallpaper still in its squares. Dust crossed the street and settled on the plane tree. At the barrier it covered them both. Something white went up in it and came down more slowly, turning, to settle at the curb.

They stayed to the end, until the arm cut out and only rubble remained.

Then she went still in another way, and looked at something near her feet.

He looked first at her, then to where she was looking.

A photograph, face up. A man stood on a step with a child at knee height beside him. Behind them was the doorframe with its first pencil line.

The bag stayed on his shoulder.

There was dust on the new leaves.
