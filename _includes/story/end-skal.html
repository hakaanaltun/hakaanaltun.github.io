They reached the corner at first light, his mother a step ahead where the pavement narrowed. They stood across from the barrier as the machine started.

She stood with her hands behind her back and her chin level, watching the wall.

The arm swung and the top floor gave. The falling walls exposed rooms with wallpaper still hanging inside. Dust crossed the street and settled on the plane tree. It covered the barrier and their clothes. Something white rose in the dust. It turned slowly as it fell to the curb.

They stayed until the machine stopped and the block was a heap of rubble.

Then his mother went still, looking at something near her feet.

He looked at her, then followed her gaze.

A photograph lay face up. In it, a man stood on a step with a child beside him at knee height. Behind them, the doorframe bore its first pencil line.

The bag stayed on his shoulder.

There was dust on the new leaves.
