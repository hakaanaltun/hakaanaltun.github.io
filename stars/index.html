The Stars

Reading your sky…

Look up. North is at the top, east on the left. The edge is the horizon; the center is directly overhead.

What is above you

The map opens at your current time and follows your clock. To explore later today, move the time slider. Press Now to return to the live sky.

This is a selection of bright stars, down to magnitude 3, with the five planets visible to the unaided eye. Only objects above the horizon are drawn. The constellation list names the regions containing the highest bright stars, with a star to look for in each.

Daylight, clouds, moonlight and city lights affect what you can see. Near the horizon, buildings and hills may hide an object. A planet close to the sun can be lost in its glare even after sunset.

Your device’s time zone chooses a nearby city. You can change it above. Star positions come from the Yale Bright Star Catalogue; coordinates are adjusted for the date. Planet positions and coordinate transformations use Astronomy Engine. Everything runs in your browser.
