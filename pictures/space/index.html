Recognizable worlds and the direction of sunlight continue across the views. These are scientific illustrations with simplified features. Sizes, distances and viewpoints are arranged for exploration; they do not show a live sky, an exact surface map or a spacecraft flight path. The ISS is enlarged so its structure can be seen. Martian relief is emphasized to make the volcanoes and canyon visible. Io, Europa, Titan, Enceladus, Miranda and Triton are enlarged separately to show their surfaces; their sizes and separations are not to scale. Jupiter’s cloud patterns are an interpretation of a changing atmosphere. Saturn’s ring structure and Enceladus’s fractures are simplified; the moon views are not surface maps. Uranus’s faint rings are shown with enhanced contrast, and Miranda’s terrain is simplified from Voyager images. The direction of its rings suggests Uranus’s orientation; this view does not measure its tilt against an orbital plane. Neptune’s weather features recall Voyager 2’s 1989 observations, with contrast strengthened for exploration. The Great Dark Spot shown here later disappeared. Triton’s terrain and colors are interpreted from a Voyager mosaic; its small dark streaks represent windblown deposits. Pluto and Charon are separately arranged portraits of the hemispheres photographed by New Horizons in 2015.
