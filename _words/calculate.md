To calculate is to work something out with numbers. The Latin
<span lang="la">*calculare*</span> meant to reckon, and it was built on
<span lang="la">*calculus*</span>, a small pebble, from
<span lang="la">*calx*</span>, limestone.{% include word-cite.html n="1" %}

The pebbles were counters. On a Roman counting board they were moved along
grooves to hold the figures of a sum.{% include word-cite.html n="1,2" %}

English took *calculate* in the 1560s. A century later *calculus* became the
name of a method in mathematics.{% include word-cite.html n="1" %}

Medicine kept the stone. Since the early eighteenth century a calculus has
been a stone formed in the body, such as a kidney stone, and dentists use the
word for hardened plaque.{% include word-cite.html n="1" %}

The limestone kept its name too. When Humphry Davy isolated a new metal in
1808, he called it calcium, after
<span lang="la">*calx*</span>.{% include word-cite.html n="3" %}

*She calculated how long the journey would take.*
