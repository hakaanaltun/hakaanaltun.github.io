A farce is a comedy of slammed doors and mistaken identity. Said of anything
else, it is a thing that has stopped being serious. It comes from the Latin <span lang="la">*farcire*</span>,
to stuff or cram. English borrowed it first in the fourteenth century, and it
meant forcemeat: the stuffing itself.{% include word-cite.html n="1,2" %}

The theatre took the word from the kitchen. Phrases were stuffed into the fixed
words of the liturgy at festivals, and the same word was then used for the
comic business stuffed in among the scenes of a religious
play.{% include word-cite.html n="1,2" %}

The stuffing grew into a form of its own. English borrowed the word a second
time in the sixteenth century, and that time it meant the
comedy.{% include word-cite.html n="2" %}

*The trial was a farce.*
