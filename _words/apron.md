English once had *napron*, borrowed from French *naperon*, a word for a small
cloth. In English it came to mean a covering worn over clothes to protect
them.{% include word-cite.html n="1" %}

Say “a napron” aloud. The same sounds can be divided as “an apron.” Over
time, speakers attached the initial n to the article, and the shortened
form became established. Edinburgh records from 1508 already contain the
spelling *apronis*.{% include word-cite.html n="1,2" %}

The older form survived elsewhere. The Dictionaries of the Scots Language
records *napron* and related spellings with their initial n intact, and
notes their use in northern English dialects too. The change spread
unevenly, leaving different versions of the same word in use.{% include word-cite.html n="3" %}

*She wiped the flour from her hands onto her apron.*
