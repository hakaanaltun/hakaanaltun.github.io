Noon is the middle of the day. The word comes from the Latin
<span lang="la">*nona hora*</span>, the ninth hour, one of the fixed hours of
prayer.{% include word-cite.html n="1,2" %}

Counted from around six in the morning, the ninth hour fell at about three in
the afternoon. That is where noon was in English for a long
time.{% include word-cite.html n="1,2" %}

Between the twelfth century and the fourteenth it moved to midday. Why is not
settled. Unreliable clocks, daylight that stretches and shrinks with the
northern seasons, and the fact that a day's fast ended at nones have all been
offered as reasons.{% include word-cite.html n="1,2" %}

Noon settled at twelve and stayed there. The hour it is named after is three
hours later.

*We'll meet at noon.*
