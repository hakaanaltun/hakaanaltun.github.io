Trivia are small facts of no great weight. The word is the Latin plural of
<span lang="la">*trivium*</span>, the place where three roads
meet.{% include word-cite.html n="1" %}

A crossroads is a public place. Anyone might be standing in it, and what was
said there was what anyone might say, so
<span lang="la">*trivialis*</span> came to mean public, and then ordinary, and
then of no particular importance.{% include word-cite.html n="1" %}

The medieval university used the same word for the first three of the seven
liberal arts — grammar, rhetoric and logic — the ones taken before the other
four. To be *trivial* was to belong to that first
stage.{% include word-cite.html n="1,2" %}

The sense the word has now, facts asked and answered for their own sake, is
from the 1960s and American campuses.{% include word-cite.html n="1" %}

*He is unbeatable at trivia.*
