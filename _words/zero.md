Zero is nothing, written down. The word goes back to the Arabic
<span lang="ar">*sifr*</span>, empty, which was itself used to render the
Sanskrit <span lang="sa">*śūnya*</span>, empty—the name Indian
mathematicians gave the mark that held an empty
place.{% include word-cite.html n="1,2" %}

Europe took the word twice. One route latinised it as
<span lang="la">*cifra*</span>, which gave English *cipher*; the other, through
Fibonacci's <span lang="la">*zephirum*</span>, gave Italian
<span lang="it">*zefiro*</span> and then
*zero*.{% include word-cite.html n="1,2,3" %}

So the two are the same word arriving by different roads. *Cipher* carried
both senses into English: zero, in its older use, and a
code.{% include word-cite.html n="3" %}

*The total came to zero.*
