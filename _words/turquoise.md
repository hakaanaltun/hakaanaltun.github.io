Turquoise is a blue-green stone, and since the nineteenth century a colour as
well. The name comes from the Old French
<span lang="fr">*turqueise*</span>, Turkish, in the phrase
<span lang="fr">*pierre turqueise*</span>: the Turkish
stone.{% include word-cite.html n="1" %}

The stone was not Turkish. The finest of it was mined in Persia, and Europe
knew it because Venetian merchants bought it in Turkish markets and the
overland routes ran through Ottoman
territory.{% include word-cite.html n="1,2" %}

So the name records the way it came rather than where it came from. The colour
was named after the stone in the 1850s, and the colour is now what most people
mean by the word: a country's name, on a paint
chart.{% include word-cite.html n="1" %}

*The ring was set with turquoise.*
