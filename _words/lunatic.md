A lunatic is a madman, in the loose and old-fashioned way the word is used now.
It comes from the Latin <span lang="la">*luna*</span>, moon, through the Late
Latin <span lang="la">*lunaticus*</span>,
moon-struck.{% include word-cite.html n="1" %}

The idea behind it was a medical one: that some kinds of madness came and went
with the phases of the moon, and that the moon was what moved
them.{% include word-cite.html n="1" %}

The belief went, and the word stayed in the law long after it. English
statutes used *lunatic* as a legal category into the twentieth century, and it
was the Mental Treatment Act of 1930 that turned lunatics, officially, into
patients.{% include word-cite.html n="2" %}

Nobody who uses the word now is thinking about the moon.

*Some lunatic overtook us on the bend.*
