To eavesdrop is to listen to something you are not part of. The word was a
place before it was an act.{% include word-cite.html n="1" %}

The eavesdrop, or eavesdrip, was the strip of ground around a building where
the rain came off the eaves. Old English had it as
<span lang="ang">*yfesdrype*</span>, and it mattered in law: you could not
build too near your neighbour's boundary, because your roof would drain onto
their land.{% include word-cite.html n="1,2" %}

Anyone standing in that strip is standing against the wall, under the eaves,
close enough to hear what is said inside. By the fifteenth century that person
had a name. The verb came later, out of the
name.{% include word-cite.html n="1,2" %}

*He was eavesdropping on the next table.*
