To meander is to wind slowly, as a river does, or a person with nowhere in
particular to be. The word is the name of a river: Greek
<span lang="grc">*Maiandros*</span>, Latin
<span lang="la">*Maeander*</span>.{% include word-cite.html n="1" %}

The river still runs. It is the <span lang="tr">Büyük Menderes</span>, the
longest river in western Türkiye, which once divided the ancient regions of
Caria and Lydia.{% include word-cite.html n="2" %}

It was known for its bends. Strabo, writing about two thousand years ago, said
its course was so winding that everything winding was called after
it.{% include word-cite.html n="3" %} Greeks already used the river’s name for
winding patterns.{% include word-cite.html n="1" %}

English took the noun in the 1570s, first for an intricate confusion and from
the 1590s for the bends of a river. The verb followed in the 1610s for a
river’s winding flow, and by the 1830s a person could meander
too.{% include word-cite.html n="1" %}

*We meandered along the shore with no plan.*
