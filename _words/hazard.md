A hazard is a danger, a risk that something will go wrong. In English it was
first the name of a game of dice, around 1300. The French
<span lang="fro">*hasard*</span> meant the game and also a throw of
six.{% include word-cite.html n="1,2" %}

The word may have come into French from Arabic. Merriam-Webster traces it
through Spanish <span lang="es">*azar*</span> to Arabic
<span lang="ar">*az-zahr*</span>, the die, probably borrowed during the
Crusades.{% include word-cite.html n="2" %}

That origin is doubted. The Online Etymology Dictionary notes that *zahr*
does not appear in the classical Arabic dictionaries, and records Ernest
Klein’s suggestion of <span lang="ar">*yasara*</span>, he played at dice,
instead.{% include word-cite.html n="1" %}

The meaning moved from the throw to the chances of life. Since the 1540s a
hazard in English has been a risk of loss or
harm.{% include word-cite.html n="1" %}

*The ice on the steps is a hazard.*
