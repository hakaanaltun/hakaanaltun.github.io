Sherbet is now a frozen dessert or, in Britain, a fizzy sweet powder. The
word is the Turkish <span lang="tr">*şerbet*</span>, a drink, from Persian
<span lang="fa">*sharbat*</span> and Arabic
<span lang="ar">*sharba*</span>, from the verb
<span lang="ar">*shariba*</span>, to drink.{% include word-cite.html n="1" %}
In Arabic, *sharba* was a single draught, the amount drunk at one
time.{% include word-cite.html n="2" %}

English met the drink in the 1580s and spelled it many ways, among them
*sorbet*, *zerbet* and *cherbet*.{% include word-cite.html n="1" %}

The same Arabic root had reached English long before, by another road.
*Syrup* came in the late fourteenth century through Old French
<span lang="fro">*sirop*</span>, from Arabic
<span lang="ar">*sharāb*</span>, a drink or
wine.{% include word-cite.html n="1" %}

The frozen sense arrived through Italian
<span lang="it">*sorbetto*</span> in the eighteenth century, and in the
nineteenth English took the French *sorbet* for it
again.{% include word-cite.html n="1" %}

*They drank cold sherbet in the shade.*
