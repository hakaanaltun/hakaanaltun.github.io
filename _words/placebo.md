A placebo can be an inactive preparation given in a medical study.
The word once belonged to a prayer.{% include word-cite.html n="1" %}

In Latin, <span lang="la">*placebo*</span> means I shall please. It
began a verse sung in the evening service for the dead, and the
service came to be called by that first word.{% include word-cite.html n="1,2" %}

In medieval English, to *sing placebo* could mean to flatter someone.
By the eighteenth century, physicians used the word for something
given to please a patient rather than for a specific therapeutic
action.{% include word-cite.html n="1,2" %}

Placebos later became important in controlled studies, where they
help researchers distinguish the effects of a treatment from effects
associated with receiving care and expecting improvement. A word from
the service for the dead became part of the language of testing
medicine.{% include word-cite.html n="1" %}

*Half the volunteers received a placebo.*
