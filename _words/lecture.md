A lecture is a talk given in order to teach something. The word comes from the
Medieval Latin <span lang="la">*lectura*</span>, a reading, from
<span lang="la">*legere*</span>, to read.{% include word-cite.html n="1,2" %}

It meant that literally. In the medieval university the teacher read out of the
book and the students wrote it down, because there was one book, it was
expensive, and they did not have it.{% include word-cite.html n="1" %}

The sense of a discourse on a subject, rather than a reading out of a text,
appears in the 1530s. Printing had been at work for two generations by
then.{% include word-cite.html n="1" %}

A lecture is now an exposition, and often anything but a reading. The word
still names the older arrangement: a room with one copy of the book in
it.{% include word-cite.html n="1,2" %}

*The lecture was at nine on Monday.*
