A freelance worker takes on work without a lasting commitment to one
employer. The word first named a hired warrior. The lance was a weapon,
and its bearer could offer his service to whoever would pay.
{% include word-cite.html n="1" %}

The word appeared in the early nineteenth century. Merriam-Webster’s
earliest written evidence comes from Walter Scott’s *Ivanhoe*, where a
lord speaks of his “Free Lances” after offering their services to Richard.
Scott was writing about medieval life long after the period itself.
{% include word-cite.html n="1" %}

The expression widened to include politicians who belonged to no party
and people who worked independently. By the late nineteenth century it
was being applied to journalism. The modern adjective keeps the idea of
offering a skill without committing to a single employer.
{% include word-cite.html n="1,2" %}

*She works as a freelance editor.*
