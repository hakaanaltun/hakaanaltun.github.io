Chauvinism is a blind belief that one’s own group is better than others.
The word is French, <span lang="fr">*chauvinisme*</span>, and it is named
after Nicolas Chauvin, a soldier who stayed devoted to Napoleon long after
the Empire had ended.{% include word-cite.html n="1,2" %}

Chauvin is known as a character. He appears in *La Cocarde tricolore*, a
popular comic play by the brothers Théodore and Hippolyte Cogniard, staged in
1831.{% include word-cite.html n="1,2" %}

Whether a real Chauvin stood behind him has never been settled. The name was
common in Napoleon’s army, and an 1822 memoir mentions a Chauvin who came
back with Napoleon from Elba, but no one has shown that he was the
man.{% include word-cite.html n="1" %}

English took *chauvinism* in 1840 for excessive patriotism. *Male
chauvinist* was in use by 1936 and became common after
1969.{% include word-cite.html n="1" %}

*The speech was full of national chauvinism.*
