To decimate a group is to kill or destroy a large part of it. The word holds
a more exact number from an older use: ten.{% include word-cite.html n="1" %}

The Latin <span lang="la">*decimare*</span> meant to punish every tenth
person. Accounts of Roman military discipline describe soldiers chosen by
lot, one in ten, from a disgraced unit and put to death.{% include word-cite.html n="1,2" %}

That does not mean Roman commanders routinely killed a tenth of their men.
The evidence for its use in the Republic is sparse, and a study of the
practice argues that it was revived in the late Republic after long periods
without it.{% include word-cite.html n="2" %}

Today *decimate* usually means to devastate, even when the loss is much more
than ten percent. The tenth remains in the word, though it is no longer a
rule for using it.{% include word-cite.html n="1" %}

*The disease decimated the herd.*
