A tragedy is a terrible event, and before that it was a kind of play with a
sorrowful end. The Greek <span lang="grc">*tragōidia*</span> is made of
<span lang="grc">*tragos*</span>, a goat, and
<span lang="grc">*ōidē*</span>, a song.{% include word-cite.html n="1" %}

Why a goat is not known. One explanation looks to satyr plays, whose
performers wore goatskins. Another looks to a singer who competed for a goat
as a prize. Some doubt the goat
altogether.{% include word-cite.html n="1" %} Aristotle wrote that tragedy
began with the authors of the dithyramb and grew away from the grotesque
manner of the satyric form.{% include word-cite.html n="2" %}

English took the word in the late fourteenth century for a serious story
that ends in misery. Chaucer, translating Boethius, defined it as prosperity
that ends in wretchedness. By about 1500 a tragedy could be any
calamity.{% include word-cite.html n="1" %}

*The fire was a tragedy for the whole street.*
