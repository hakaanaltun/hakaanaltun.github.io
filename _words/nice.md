Calling someone nice is usually a compliment. When the word reached English
from French in the late thirteenth century, it could mean foolish or
ignorant. Behind it was Latin <span lang="la">*nescius*</span>, meaning
unaware or not knowing.{% include word-cite.html n="1" %}

The change took centuries. Among its later meanings were fussy and hard to
please, then delicate and precise. Something nice could require careful
attention or a discriminating taste. By the eighteenth century the word
was also being used for things that pleased, and later for people who were
kind or considerate.{% include word-cite.html n="1" %}

Some of those meanings still coexist. A *nice distinction* is a subtle or
precise distinction; someone with a *nice palate* is particular about what
they taste. These uses preserve the exacting sense alongside the much
broader approval in *a nice day*.{% include word-cite.html n="2" %}

*It was nice of her to wait for us.*
