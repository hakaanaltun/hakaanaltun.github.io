To call someone a dunce is to call them slow to learn. The word comes from
John Duns Scotus, a medieval philosopher admired for the subtlety of his
thinking.{% include word-cite.html n="1" %}

Scotus studied and taught at major European universities. His lectures on
Peter Lombard's *Sentences*, a central work of medieval theology, became a
widely read commentary. Glasgow University Library holds a printed edition
from 1477, nearly 170 years after his death.{% include word-cite.html n="2" %}

His reputation changed as humanism gained ground in the sixteenth century.
Followers who defended his teaching were ridiculed for resisting the new
learning. *Duns*, a name associated with a school of thought, became a term
of contempt. It acquired the spelling *dunce* and broadened into an insult
for someone considered incapable of learning.{% include word-cite.html n="1,2" %}

*He felt like a dunce when he finally understood the instructions.*
