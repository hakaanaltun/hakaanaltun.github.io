A saturnine person is gloomy and slow to warm. The word means “of Saturn,”
and in the fifteenth century it described someone born under that planet’s
influence.{% include word-cite.html n="1,2" %}

Saturn was thought to be the planet farthest from the Sun, and so the coldest
and the slowest. Old medicine held that people born under it took on its
nature.{% include word-cite.html n="1" %}

Renaissance writers gave the planet a second side. Marsilio Ficino, himself
born under Saturn, tied it to melancholy and to the deep thought of scholars,
so that a child of Saturn could be gloomy and gifted at
once.{% include word-cite.html n="3" %}

Saturn was also the alchemists’ name for lead, so *saturnine* can mean “of
lead.” Medicine still uses it that way: saturnine gout is caused by lead
poisoning.{% include word-cite.html n="1,2" %}

*He gave a saturnine smile and said nothing.*
