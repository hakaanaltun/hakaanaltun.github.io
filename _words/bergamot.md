Bergamot is a small citrus fruit with a fragrant, bitter oil in its
rind.{% include word-cite.html n="1" %} Where its name comes from is not
settled.

The word first named a pear. In the 1610s English used *bergamot* for an
especially fine pear, and the Online Etymology Dictionary traces that name to
the Turkish <span lang="tr">*bey armudu*</span>, the bey’s pear, reshaped in
Italian to sound like the town of Bergamo.{% include word-cite.html n="2" %}

For the citrus, which came into English in the 1690s, the same dictionary
takes the town itself: Bergamo, in northern
Italy.{% include word-cite.html n="2" %} Merriam-Webster gives the citrus the
Turkish pear instead.{% include word-cite.html n="1" %}

Both dictionaries find the Turkish pear in the word. They disagree about
whether the citrus owes its name to the pear or to the town.

*The tea smelled of bergamot.*
