A vaccine is a preparation that trains the body against a disease. The word
comes from the Latin <span lang="la">*vacca*</span>,
cow.{% include word-cite.html n="1,2" %}

Edward Jenner's report of 1798 was an inquiry into the
<span lang="la">*Variolae vaccinae*</span>, the pustules of the cow: his term
for cowpox. People who had caught cowpox, usually from milking, turned out to
be protected against smallpox.{% include word-cite.html n="1" %}

The method spread and the name went with it. Vaccines are made in many ways
now and almost none of them involve a cow, but the word still says where the
first one was found.{% include word-cite.html n="1,2" %}

*The vaccine was given in two doses.*
