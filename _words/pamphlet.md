The word comes from *Pamphilus, seu de Amore*, a popular Latin poem about
love written in the twelfth century. Copies circulated as small booklets,
and a form of the title gradually became a name for short works of this
kind. Pamphilus was the central character's name.{% include word-cite.html n="1,2" %}

The word was already in use for handwritten works before printing reached
England. Printed pamphlets later carried political arguments and news,
often cheaply enough to reach readers who could afford few books.{% include word-cite.html n="2" %}

The format also served fiction. Dickens's *David Copperfield* appeared in
monthly parts bound in paper wrappers. Those installments belong to the
history of pamphlet binding too. A small folded publication could carry a
public dispute or another portion of a novel whose readers were still
waiting to learn what happened next.{% include word-cite.html n="3" %}

*Someone had left a pamphlet on every seat.*
