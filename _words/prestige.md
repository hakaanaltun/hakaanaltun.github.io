Prestige is the standing someone has in other people’s eyes. When English
borrowed the word from French in the seventeenth century, it meant a trick
or illusion. Its Latin ancestry belongs to conjuring and deception.
{% include word-cite.html n="1,2" %}

In the nineteenth century the word began to describe the impressive
appearance of someone’s importance or ability. It could still suggest a
flattering illusion, but it also came to mean the reputation itself.
Admiration became part of the word’s ordinary meaning.
{% include word-cite.html n="1" %}

*Prestigious* had entered English separately, from a related Latin word
meaning deceitful or full of tricks. It kept that sense for centuries
before acquiring its familiar meaning of highly regarded around the
beginning of the twentieth century. Both words had moved from deception
to esteem.{% include word-cite.html n="2" %}

*The discovery brought considerable prestige to the university.*
