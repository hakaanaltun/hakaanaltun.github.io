A juggernaut is a force that flattens whatever is in front of it. The word is
an English rendering of Jagannath, a deity of eastern India whose name means
lord of the universe.{% include word-cite.html n="1" %}

One part of the image is plain enough. At the Rath Yatra in Puri, enormous
chariots carrying the deities are pulled through the streets by hundreds of
people, and something that heavy is genuinely hard to stop or steer once it is
moving.{% include word-cite.html n="1" %}

The rest was supplied. Claudius Buchanan, an Anglican chaplain who had seen the
festival, published *Christian Researches in Asia* in 1811; it became one of
the most widely read English accounts of India of its day. He wrote the
procession up as a scene of heathen horror, in support of the case for
Christian missions, and that is the book the English word came
through.{% include word-cite.html n="2" %}

English kept the crushing and left the rest behind.{% include word-cite.html n="1,2" %}

*The company became a juggernaut.*
