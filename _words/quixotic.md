A quixotic plan is driven by an ideal that may be impossible to carry out.
The adjective comes from a character in a book.{% include word-cite.html n="1" %}

Don Quixote, in Miguel de Cervantes's novel, sets out to live as a knight.
He sees a world of quests and enemies where other people see ordinary things.
When he spots windmills on a plain, he takes them for giants. Sancho Panza
tells him what they are, but Quixote charges them anyway. After he falls, he
decides an enchanter must have turned the giants into windmills.{% include word-cite.html n="2" %}

His name first became a word for a person like him, *a Quixote*, and then an
adjective. *Quixotic* can admire someone's commitment to a noble aim while
questioning whether the aim can be reached. It can also describe the moment
someone's idea of the world stops them from seeing what is in front of
them.{% include word-cite.html n="1,2" %}

*The plan to restore every abandoned house was quixotic.*
