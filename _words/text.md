A text has a history of weaving. The Latin verb *texere* meant to weave or
join things together. From it came *textus*, something woven, which was
also used for the style or texture of a work.{% include word-cite.html n="1" %}

The image passed into writing. Late Latin used *textus* for a written
account, and medieval usage included the Scriptures and other written
works. English acquired *text* through French in the late fourteenth
century, when it could mean the wording of something written or an
authoritative passage.{% include word-cite.html n="1" %}

*Textile* comes from the same Latin verb, through *textilis*, meaning woven.
The relationship between the two English words is easy to overlook because
one belongs to writing and the other to fabric. Both retain the older idea
of separate strands being joined into something continuous.{% include word-cite.html n="2" %}
