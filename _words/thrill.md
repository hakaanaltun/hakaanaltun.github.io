A thrill is a sudden rush of excitement or fear that seems to go right
through you. The word began with exactly that. The Old English verb
<span lang="ang">*þyrlian*</span> meant to pierce, and it came from
<span lang="ang">*þyrel*</span>, a hole. In Middle English
<span lang="enm">*thirlen*</span> became *thrillen*, as two of its sounds
changed places.{% include word-cite.html n="1" %}

By the late fourteenth century the verb could be used of a feeling that
pierces someone. The shivering, exciting feeling is recorded from the 1590s,
and the noun from the 1670s.{% include word-cite.html n="1" %}

The same hole is still in *nostril*. Old English
<span lang="ang">*nosþyrl*</span> was the hole of the nose, from
<span lang="ang">*nosu*</span>, nose, and
<span lang="ang">*þyrel*</span>.{% include word-cite.html n="2" %}

*A thrill went through the crowd when the doors opened.*
