On November 24, 1762, Edward Gibbon recorded an evening at a London club
where men were eating sandwiches. His journal provides an early written
record of the food's English name, already familiar enough to use without
explaining it.{% include word-cite.html n="1" %}

The name is traced to John Montagu, the fourth Earl of Sandwich. The
familiar account has him eating beef between slices of bread so that he
could carry on gambling without leaving the table. It comes from the
French travel writer Pierre-Jean Grosley, who visited London in 1765 and
later described a minister spending twenty-four hours at a game with
little else to eat.{% include word-cite.html n="2" %}

That account is difficult to confirm. Grosley was passing on a story, and
the evidence for Montagu as a compulsive gambler is weak. His biographer
N. A. M. Rodger suggested that eating while working at his desk might
explain the association instead. That remains a suggestion
too.{% include word-cite.html n="2" %}

The English name has a much firmer history than the famous card game.
Gibbon's entry shows it in use before Grosley's visit. We can date that
written record; the meal that first attached the earl's title to the food
is harder to recover.{% include word-cite.html n="1,2" %}
