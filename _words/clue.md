A clue is something that helps you find an answer. It was once a *clew*, a
ball of thread or yarn, from the Old English
<span lang="ang">*cliewen*</span>, a ball or
skein.{% include word-cite.html n="1" %}

The change came from a Greek myth. Ariadne gave Theseus a thread to take into
the Labyrinth, and once he had killed the Minotaur he followed it back out.
Ovid tells the story in the *Metamorphoses*.{% include word-cite.html n="2" %}

From the 1590s English used the word for anything that guides a person
through an intricate case, and by the 1620s for whatever points the way, with
no labyrinth involved.{% include word-cite.html n="1" %}

The spelling changed as well. Middle English often wrote French words ending
in *-ue* with *-ew*, and when their spelling went back to *-ue*, some native
words followed, among them *hue*, *true* and *clue*. The spelling *clue*
appears in the middle of the fifteenth century, and *clew* survived for the
thread in northern English and Scots.{% include word-cite.html n="1" %}

*There was no clue to who had written the letter.*
