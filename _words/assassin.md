An assassin kills someone prominent, usually for political reasons. The word
came into European languages from variants of the Arabic
<span lang="ar">*hashishi*</span>, plural
<span lang="ar">*hashishiyya*</span>.{% include word-cite.html n="1" %}

It was not a description. Other Muslims used it against the Nizari Ismailis as
an insult, in the sense of low-class rabble or people of lax morals, with no
claim attached that they used the drug.{% include word-cite.html n="1" %}

The literal reading came later, and from outside. Crusader-era reports and then
Marco Polo told of an Old Man of the Mountain who drugged young men and showed
them a garden of paradise so they would kill on command. Farhad Daftary's work
on those accounts traces them to European invention and rumour rather than to
anything recorded at Alamut.{% include word-cite.html n="1,2" %}

The legend is what crossed into English, and the word came with it. The drug it
looks like it names is the part no source supports.{% include word-cite.html n="1,2" %}

*He was killed by an assassin.*
