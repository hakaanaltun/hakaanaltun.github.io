A kiosk is a small stall you buy something at, or a screen you touch to print a
ticket. It came into English through the French
<span lang="fr">*kiosque*</span>, from the Turkish
<span lang="tr">*köşk*</span>, and behind that the Persian
<span lang="fa">*kūshk*</span>.{% include word-cite.html n="1" %}

A köşk was a pavilion, open on some or all sides, built in a garden to be sat
in. The Çinili Köşk at Topkapı, put up in 1472 under Mehmed II, was a pleasure
pavilion in the outer gardens, used to watch the games and spectacles held in
the square it faced.{% include word-cite.html n="2" %}

Europe borrowed the building first, in the seventeenth century, as ornament for
parks and gardens. Then the word was handed down to smaller and smaller things:
a newspaper stand by the 1860s, a telephone box in the 1920s, and now a machine
bolted to the floor of an airport.{% include word-cite.html n="1" %}

*She bought a ticket at the kiosk.*
