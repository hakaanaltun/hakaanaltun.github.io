A divan is a low sofa, usually without a back. The word began in Persian,
where <span lang="fa">*devan*</span> meant a bundle of written sheets or a
small book.{% include word-cite.html n="1" %}

In Arabic, <span lang="ar">*dīwān*</span> moved from a book of accounts to the
office that kept them, and from there to a government office and the council
that met in its chamber.{% include word-cite.html n="1,2" %} English first
took the word from Turkish in the 1580s, for a council of
state.{% include word-cite.html n="1" %}

The seat came from the room. The long cushioned benches along the walls of a
council chamber were called divans, and English has used the word for a sofa
since 1702.{% include word-cite.html n="1" %}

Two other senses went their own way. A divan is also a poet’s collected
poems, as in the *Divan* of Hafez.{% include word-cite.html n="1,2" %} In
Italy the Arabic word became <span lang="it">*dogana*</span>, earlier
<span lang="it">*doana*</span>, the customs
house.{% include word-cite.html n="3" %}

*He fell asleep on the divan with the book still open.*
