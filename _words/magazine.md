A magazine is something you read. The word comes from the Arabic
<span lang="ar">*makhzan*</span>, a storehouse or depot, and reached English
through French in the 1580s.{% include word-cite.html n="1" %}

Its first English sense was the literal one: a warehouse, and particularly a
store of military supplies and ammunition.{% include word-cite.html n="1" %}

In 1731 Edward Cave started a monthly in London called *The Gentleman's
Magazine*, made up of pieces gathered from other publications. He meant the
word as it sounds, a storehouse of writing, and it is the first periodical the
word was used for.{% include word-cite.html n="1,2" %}

The rifle kept the older sense. The part that holds the cartridges is a
magazine because it is where they are stored. The shelf at the newsagent and
the box of ammunition are named for the same
thing.{% include word-cite.html n="1" %}
